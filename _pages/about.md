I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Political Science at the University of Massachusetts Amherst, working at the intersection of international relations, law and society, and human rights. My research asks how survivors of historical injustice and their allies mobilize law across borders, and what that mobilization reveals about the possibilities and limits of court-based justice for conflict-related sexual violence.

My dissertation examines the transnational reparations lawsuits brought by survivors of Japanese military sexual slavery (the "comfort women" issue) in Korean, Japanese, and U.S. courts. Drawing on more than two years of multi-sited fieldwork in South Korea (2022–2024), archival research, and interviews, I trace the decentralized yet determined support structure of lawyers, activists, scholars, and survivors that has sustained litigation despite repeated legal defeats. The project speaks to broader debates on legal mobilization, victim agency, postcolonial politics, and transitional justice. You can read more on the [Research](/research/) page.

My work has been supported by the American Association of University Women (AAUW) International Doctoral Degree Fellowship, the UMass Graduate School Dissertation Completion Fellowship, and the Research Enhancement and Leadership (REAL) Fellowship. I have published in *Society and History* and *Governance*, contributed a chapter to an edited volume from the Northeast Asian History Foundation (2026), and presented my work at the American Political Science Association, the International Studies Association, the Law and Society Association, and the Asian Law and Society Association. In 2024–25 I was a Graduate Fellow at the UMass Center for Justice, Law, and Society.

I teach Globalization and the Law (LEGAL 343) as the instructor of record and have served as a teaching assistant for courses in constitutional law, bias and the legal system, U.S. foreign policy, and world politics.

Before coming to UMass, I earned an M.A. in Sociology from Seoul National University and a B.A. in Sociology (magna cum laude) from Ewha Womans University, and worked as a researcher at the Korean Women's Development Institute and the Korean Educational Development Institute.
